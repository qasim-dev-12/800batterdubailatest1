Car Battery Warranty in the UAE: What's Covered and How Claims Work

A battery warranty is only as useful as its terms. This guide explains free-replacement and pro-rata cover, the common reasons a claim is refused, what paperwork to keep, and how we handle a warranty claim at your car in Dubai.

When you compare battery quotes in Dubai, the warranty is often the line that separates two similar prices. One quote says 12 months, another says 24, and a third simply says "warranty included". The length matters, but so does what the warranty actually promises and what you need to do to claim on it.

Owners usually read the terms for the first time when a battery has already failed, often on a hot morning in a basement car park. By then it is too late to keep the receipt you threw away or to ask whether the cover was a straight replacement or a partial credit.

This guide explains the two common types of battery warranty, the reasons claims are refused, the documents worth keeping, and how a claim works when we fitted the battery. Manufacturer and distributor terms vary from brand to brand, so always read the warranty card that comes with your battery.

What a car battery warranty covers

A battery warranty covers a manufacturing defect: a battery that fails to hold charge or deliver its rated starting power under normal use, within the warranty period. It is a promise about the battery itself, not about everything that can make a car not start.

It does not normally cover a battery that has simply been run flat, a battery damaged by a fault elsewhere in the car, or a battery fitted to a car it was never specified for. That distinction is why almost every warranty claim starts with a test rather than an automatic swap.

The warranty period usually runs from the date of purchase or fitting shown on your invoice, not from the date the battery was made. Keep that date somewhere you can find it.

Free replacement vs pro-rata warranty

Battery warranties tend to fall into two types, and some combine both: a free-replacement period first, followed by a pro-rata period.

The two common warranty structures
TypeHow it worksWhat you pay at claimWatch for
Free replacementA battery confirmed as faulty within the period is replaced with a new oneUsually nothing for the battery itselfWhether fitting or a call-out is included, and whether the replacement starts a new warranty
Pro-rataYou receive credit for the unused part of the warranty towards a new batteryThe used share of the battery's priceHow the credit is calculated, and whether it is based on the original or current price

If a quote only gives a number of months, ask whether that period is free replacement throughout or partly pro-rata.

What can void a battery warranty

Most refused claims come down to a handful of causes. None of them is a trick; they are situations where the battery did not fail because of how it was made.

  • Wrong application: a standard flooded battery fitted to a stop-start car that needs EFB or AGM, or a battery with too little capacity or cold cranking amps for the engine.
  • Deep discharge: a battery left completely flat for a long period, for example during a summer away, can be permanently damaged. Repeated deep discharge is usually treated as use, not a defect.
  • Physical damage: a cracked case, broken terminal, bulging from overheating, or damage from a loose hold-down.
  • Charging system faults: an alternator that overcharges or undercharges, or a parasitic drain from an accessory, will shorten any battery's life.
  • Tampering or misuse: opened sealed caps, added acid, or a battery used for something other than starting a car.
  • Missing proof of purchase: without an invoice or registered warranty, the start date cannot be confirmed.

A failed claim is not always bad news. If the test shows a charging fault, you have found the real reason the battery died before buying another one that would go the same way.

Why a claim is tested before a battery is replaced

A flat battery and a faulty battery look the same from the driver's seat: slow cranking, clicking, or no power at all. The only way to tell them apart is to charge and test the battery and check the car around it.

A proper warranty check usually covers the battery's state of charge and its ability to deliver current under load, the charging voltage with the engine running, and signs of drain or physical damage. If the battery passes after charging, the problem lies elsewhere and a new battery would not fix it.

Testing protects you as well as the seller. It stops a good battery being swapped for no reason and points to the fault that would otherwise kill the next battery.

Documents to keep

A claim is much simpler when you can show what was fitted and when. Keep these together, ideally as photos on your phone:

  • The invoice or receipt showing the date, the battery brand and model, and the seller.
  • The warranty card or registration confirmation, if the brand or seller issues one.
  • A clear photo of the battery label, including any date code or serial number.
  • Records of any earlier tests, jump starts or charging system repairs.

Some brands and distributors register warranties against a phone number or a serial number. The warranty card that comes with the battery explains how that brand handles it.

Comparing warranties when you get a quote

When two quotes differ mainly on warranty, a few questions make the comparison fair:

  • Is the period free replacement throughout, or does part of it become pro-rata?
  • Does a claim include the visit and fitting, or only the battery?
  • Who do you contact to claim: the seller, the brand's distributor, or a service centre?
  • Is the battery the correct type and size for the car? A warranty will not help if the battery was the wrong one to begin with.

Questions

How long is a car battery warranty in the UAE?

It depends on the brand, the range and the seller. When we supply and fit a battery, our warranty is 24 months for Bosch, Varta, Panasonic and Mobis, 18 months for Amaron, ACDelco, Energizer, Tudor, Duracell and Fiamm, and 12 months for Exide, Solite and other value brands. Your quote confirms the exact term.

Does a flat battery count as a warranty fault?

Not on its own. A battery that has been run flat by lights left on or a long period parked is usually fine once charged. A claim is accepted when the charged battery fails a test under normal conditions.

How do I check my Amaron or Bosch battery warranty?

Start with your invoice and the warranty card that came with the battery. The card explains how that brand registers and handles claims. If we fitted it, send us the invoice details and we will check it for you.

What happens if my claim is refused?

The test result shows why. Often it points to a charging system fault, a drain or the wrong battery type, which is worth fixing before any new battery goes in.

Do I need to keep the old receipt?

Yes. The invoice proves the start date and what was fitted. A photo of it on your phone is enough in most cases.
